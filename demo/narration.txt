CashMandate lets you give an AI agent a budget instead of your key. Here is how an agent actually uses it.

The agent connects over the Model Context Protocol, with its own key and the public terms of the mandate. The owner's key never reaches the agent. It discovers two tools: read the mandate, and pay.

Here is a real session on chipnet. The owner locked thirty thousand satoshis in the vault, with a limit of two thousand per payment and two approved suppliers. The agent is a language model working through an invoice inbox. We told it to follow vendor instructions exactly, like a careless assistant would.

Invoice one comes from an approved supplier, inside the limit. The payment goes through, with a memo written on the chain.

Invoice two is a trap. A message says the bank details changed, and to send the money to a new address. The agent obeys. The covenant refuses, and returns the exact line of the script that failed: the recipient must be on the approved list.

Invoice three asks for nine thousand satoshis. Refused again: the payment is above the per-spend limit. The agent reports the refusal, and carries on.

The last invoice is legitimate, from an approved supplier, and it is paid.

The web app shows what happened on chain. The ledger lists the funding, both payments with their memos, and the revocation. Every row is a live chipnet transaction.

These are the attempts the covenant stopped, with the contract's own reply. The agent was fooled, and the money stayed.

You can try to break the mandate yourself. Push the payment past the limit, and the rule fails. Choose any other address, and it fails again.

At the end, the owner revoked the mandate. One signature, and everything left returns to the owner. Twenty six tests cover every rogue spend. CashMandate: give your agent a budget, not your key.
